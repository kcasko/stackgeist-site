# StackGeist editorial voice

**Authorial baseline:** A practical troubleshooter explaining what matters to another person who actually has the problem. Technical without sounding like a user manual; direct without being abrasive. This voice is based on the owner's chat conversations and clearly verified project/course experiences, not an assumed archive of personally authored blog posts.

## What readers should hear

- **Lead with the fix or decision.** "If Windows sees the drive in Disk Management, stop messing with USB cables. The next question is whether the partition is healthy." More useful than "In today's fast-paced digital world..."
- **Explain the why in plain English.** Show the mental model behind the step. If two things look identical but work differently, say so.
- **Tell people what not to waste time on.** If it's an app permission problem, reinstalling drivers is not the first move. If a disk may contain valuable data, stop before formatting.
- **Use specific language and realistic examples.** Real commands, realistic failure modes, what success looks like, and when to stop.
- **Be opinionated only when the evidence supports it.** "I wouldn't buy this dock for one monitor" is fine with a grounded reason. Don't make up a hardware benchmark or claim to have tested something just to sound experienced.
- **First-person only for real firsthand work.** The author's WGU, home lab, bakery background, AWS projects, and actual troubleshooting reports can support first-person claims when they are documented. Vendor-spec and Reddit-derived information stays explicitly attributed. No invented timelines, equipment ownership, customers, or test results.
- **Let humor happen sparingly.** Occasional dry wit fits. Forced jokes, profanity in every paragraph, or fake gamer references make a technical guide harder to trust.
- **Reddit is context, not proof.** A student post can show what another person found useful; label it as their experience and link to it. Never present someone else's method as the author's.
- **No AI-looking production scaffolding.** Remove headings such as "Editorial deep dive," repetitive "Key takeaways," generic "The ultimate guide," boilerplate conclusions, and strings of symmetrical cards that repeat the same information.
- **No fake human touches.** Don't insert "I tried this yesterday," dramatic personal anecdotes, fake mistakes, manufactured product photos, or unverified statements about having tested hardware.

## Structure is a tool, not a template

The article can start with a short fix box, steps, a diagram, a comparison, or a failure example depending on what the subject needs. Repeating the same kicker/section order on dozens of pages is exactly what makes a site feel machine assembled.

Technical accuracy, accessibility, attribution, affiliate disclosures, and safety are nonnegotiable even when the phrasing gets more casual.

## Editing pass before publishing

1. Read the opening aloud. Is this how the owner would explain the issue to a friend, or does it sound like a sales landing page?
2. Replace passive hedging where the evidence is clear: "It may be advisable to consider" becomes "Check".
3. Remove duplicate advice, generic filler, unnecessary summaries, and overly formal transitions.
4. Replace repetitive headings with the specific reader question answered by the section.
5. Confirm any first-person claim against an actual recorded experience; otherwise use neutral instructional language.
6. Check technical details with authoritative references and label model/OS-specific limitations.
7. Check that the article works even if the reader buys nothing.
8. Read the entire finished article, not just the paragraphs added in this batch. A new section should not repeat a previous one in different words.

## Examples

**Not StackGeist:** "This comprehensive troubleshooting journey will empower users to navigate the multifaceted complexities of USB-C connectivity."

**StackGeist:** "Ethernet works, but HDMI is black? That doesn't mean the hub is half-broken. Ethernet uses USB data. Video needs a display signal from the laptop, and not every USB-C port has one."

**Not StackGeist:** "My extensive testing shows this product is the perfect solution."

**StackGeist:** "On paper, it has the ports you need. But before buying it, check whether your laptop can actually drive two external screens. I haven't benchmarked this dock, so I won't pretend I can guarantee that."

**Not StackGeist:** "Leverage innovative educational strategies to optimize your learning outcomes."

**StackGeist:** "I kept going over the material and still missed the same questions. Things started to click when I stopped rereading everything and worked through one small example at a time."

**Source discipline:** That last first-person line is acceptable only on a course page where the author's earlier study notes support it. It is not a generic persona decoration.

## Source of voice

Short, direct chat messages provide the rhythm and opinions. Longer autobiographical details need specific backing from the author's prior conversations and project records. This is a working house style to refine as authentic published writing samples become available, not a claim of perfect voice matching.
