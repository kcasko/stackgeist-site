**Goal:** Bring every useful, published troubleshooting guide, lab, WGU/certification article and product decision page up to a defensible subject-matter standard. Do not inflate length, fabricate ownership/benchmarks, or republish a retired kit or desk-setup page.

## Definition of done

1. Verify the exact hardware model, OS version, course version, or tested environment. Label firsthand work versus sourced research.
2. Start with a direct answer, safety/data-preservation gate where relevant, and a symptom-to-cause decision path.
3. Provide reproducible steps, expected observations, safe stop conditions, and verification. Avoid commands that risk data without explicit precautions.
4. Use authoritative vendor/platform sources for claims; attribute community anecdotes to community authors. Never invent personal WGU experiences or exam content.
5. Add a diagram, screenshot or original illustration only when it teaches a concrete distinction. Screenshots must show real UI or be labeled illustrative.
6. Disclose any paid product link, give reasons not to buy, and keep advice useful without a purchase.
7. Pass static link/sitemap and structured-data tests, responsive Playwright checks, and preserve the four active site departments.
8. Prefer consolidating noindex/unverified product stubs over publishing artificial filler or unsupported endorsements.
