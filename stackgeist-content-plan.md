# Products Rejected During Initial Research

## Generic RGB headset stands

**Reason:** Many unbranded or lightly branded listings had vague electrical claims, weak manufacturer documentation, unclear warranty support, and copy that looked interchangeable. Razer Base Station V2 Chroma is a better documented alternative.

## Generic extended desk mats

**Reason:** Many had vague materials, inconsistent dimensions, weak durability documentation, and no meaningful distinction beyond a printed graphic. SteelSeries and Razer offer more verifiable specifications.

## Govee Envisual TV LED Backlight T2

**Reason:** It solves a real TV bias-lighting problem but is not central enough to desk or workstation setup content. Keep it as a future gaming-room expansion topic, not a launch priority.

## BenQ ScreenBar Halo 2 / ScreenBar Pro

**Reason:** They are valid premium options but overlap heavily with the basic ScreenBar. The original ScreenBar offers a cleaner first article with wider appeal.

## Generic rolling storage carts and charging side tables

**Reason:** Adjacent to desk organization but not focused enough on desk setup, gaming, cables, lighting, power, or accessories. Brands and specifications were also often weak.

# Product Review Template

Use this structure for every StackGeist product review.

## 1. Disclosure and quick verdict

Put the affiliate disclosure near the first affiliate link. Include:

> StackGeist may earn a commission if you buy through links in this article. This does not change what you pay.

For Amazon Associates, also use the required statement on the site:

> As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.

Verdict box fields:

- Best for
- Skip if
- The main reason to buy
- The one compatibility issue to check
- Price status: do not hardcode prices; use “check current price” links

## 2. What problem does it solve?

Begin with the real user problem, not product marketing.

Bad:

> The Anker Nano is a great power strip with innovative features.

Better:

> If your desk power strip lives on the floor, your charging cables crawl across the desktop, and you keep reaching behind furniture to plug in a controller, a clamp-mounted power hub solves that specific mess.

## 3. Verified specifications

Use a table with only confirmed specifications.

| Specification | Verified information | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Desk thickness | 0.6–1.8 in | Determines clamp compatibility |
| USB-C output | Up to 70W | Determines whether it can charge a laptop |
| Surge rating | 1,500J | Helps compare protection claims |

If a detail cannot be confirmed, write **Not verified**. Do not fill gaps with guesses.

## 4. Compatibility checklist

Use direct yes/no requirements:

- Does the desk edge fit the clamp range?
- Is the monitor within the arm’s weight capacity?
- Does the laptop USB-C port support DisplayPort Alt Mode?
- Does the monitor support the required VESA pattern?
- Is the product supported on the reader’s operating system?

## 5. Tradeoffs and skip reasons

Make the downside useful.

Bad:

> It is a little expensive.

Better:

> Skip this if your desk is glass or has a curved front edge. The clamp is not designed for that, and forcing it is a dumb way to turn a clean desk upgrade into broken furniture.

## 6. Alternatives

Offer different solutions, not fake alternatives.

- Budget option
- Better option for a specific constraint
- Premium option
- Different product category for readers whose needs differ

## 7. FAQ

Turn actual buyer concerns into headings:

- Will this fit a 1.5-inch thick desk?
- Can this charge a 14-inch laptop?
- Does this work with two monitors on a MacBook?
- Can this hold a 27-inch curved monitor?

## 8. Sources and verification date

End with:

- Manufacturer source(s)
- Retail source(s) only for sales availability and exact Amazon product identity
- Last verified date
